# Privacy Policy for Vulto

**Effective date:** 2026-08-07
**Last updated:** 2026-08-07

This Privacy Policy describes how HTBR ("we", "us", or "our") collects, uses, and shares personal information when you use the mobile application Vulto ("the App").

Vulto is a digital contact-card app: you create one or more cards holding a display name and a single contact channel (an Instagram, TikTok, LinkedIn, Meta, X, YouTube or Telegram handle, a phone number, an email address, or a custom link), and share the selected card with another person by showing them a QR code.

On Android, you can also choose to save a card to Google Wallet so its QR code is available from your wallet without opening Vulto. This is the one feature in which information you have entered is sent off your device, and only when you tap the "Add to Google Wallet" button — see Section 3.

We respect your privacy and are committed to protecting it through our compliance with this policy.

## 2. Personal information we collect

**We do not collect any personal information.**

Vulto operates on your device. The App contains no analytics, no crash-reporting, no advertising and no tracking software, has no user accounts, and does not communicate with any server operated by us. We never receive, see, or store your data — we operate no server, and nothing you enter is ever sent to us.

On Android, if you choose to save a card to Google Wallet, that card's details go from your device directly to Google, never to us. Section 3 sets out exactly what is sent, and Section 4 explains Google's role.

### Information you store on your own device

The App stores the following on your device only, so that it can display your cards:

| What is stored | Where | Who can access it |
|---|---|---|
| Card display name | Local app database on your device | You only |
| Contact value — social handle, phone number, email address, or custom link | Local app database on your device | You only |
| Card label, custom channel label, and international dialling prefix | Local app database on your device | You only |
| Card order, the identifier of the last card you viewed, and whether you have seen the first-run tips | Local app preferences on your device | You only |

This information is provided by you, is never transmitted to us, and never leaves your device except in the way you deliberately choose — see Section 3.

Because we do not collect or process this information, there is no processing by us that requires a legal basis under the GDPR, and there are no retention periods on our side. The data persists on your device until you delete the card, clear the App's data, or uninstall the App.

We do not knowingly collect any categories of personal information beyond those listed above. If you believe we have collected information not disclosed here, please contact us at htbr.app@gmail.com.

## 3. How your information is used and shared

The App uses the information you store solely to display your cards and to build the QR code and links you choose to share. All of this happens on your device.

Your information leaves your device only when **you** take one of these actions:

- **You show a QR code to another person.** Depending on the card you select, the QR code encodes the public profile URL for the chosen social channel, a standard vCard containing the display name and the phone number or email address on that card, or — for a custom card — the exact value you entered, as you entered it. Whoever scans the code receives that information directly from your screen. We are not involved in this exchange and it does not pass through any server of ours.
- **You tap a card that opens an external destination.** Social cards open the corresponding profile page (instagram.com, tiktok.com, linkedin.com, facebook.com, x.com, youtube.com, or t.me) and custom cards open the address you entered. Your device's browser or the relevant app handles the request, and that third party's own privacy policy governs what it collects. We do not send them any information about you.
- **You save a card to Google Wallet (Android only).** If you tap "Add to Google Wallet", the App builds a wallet pass from the selected card and hands it to the Google Wallet app on your device, which sends it to Google LLC to store in your Google Account's wallet. The pass contains the card's display name, its label, the channel name, the contact value on that card (handle, phone number with its dialling prefix, email address, or custom link), and the same QR code the App displays. Google receives this information as an independent controller and handles it under the Google Privacy Policy (https://policies.google.com/privacy) and the Google Wallet terms. The pass is stored in your Google Account, so it may sync to your other signed-in devices. **This step is entirely optional** — if you never tap the button, no card data is ever sent to Google. The feature is not available on iOS, where no card data is sent to any wallet provider.

  Two consequences are worth knowing before you use it. A saved pass is a copy: editing or deleting the card in Vulto does **not** change or remove a pass you already saved, because Vulto has no ability to modify or delete a pass once Google holds it. Editing a card and saving again adds a *new* pass rather than updating the old one. To remove a pass, delete it in the Google Wallet app yourself.

- **You open a legal document from the Help screen.** This Privacy Policy and our Terms of Service are hosted on GitHub Pages, a service of GitHub, Inc. (a Microsoft company, United States). Like any web host, GitHub may log your IP address and browser details as a normal part of serving the page. This happens only if you open one of these documents, and it is governed by GitHub's own privacy statement (https://docs.github.com/en/site-policy/privacy-policies/github-general-privacy-statement). We receive no information about who reads these pages.

We do not use your personal information for automated decision-making that produces legal or similarly significant effects on you.

## 4. Who we share your information with

**We do not share your personal information with anyone, because we do not collect it.**

We use no third-party processors, no analytics providers, no advertising networks, and no cloud storage or backup services. **We do not sell your personal information to anyone.** Saving a card to Google Wallet is not a sale or a share for cross-context behavioural advertising: it is a transfer you initiate, to a service you have chosen, and we receive no payment or other consideration for it.

The third-party services named in Section 3 — the social platforms whose profile pages a card can open, any address you enter yourself on a custom card, Google LLC when you save a card to Google Wallet, and GitHub, Inc. as the host of these legal documents — receive information only when you deliberately open them or, in Google's case, when you deliberately save a pass. Each acts as an independent controller in its own right, under its own privacy policy, rather than as a processor acting on our instructions. We do not send them any information about you, and we receive nothing back from them.

Because some of these services are based outside the European Economic Area, opening one — or saving a pass to Google Wallet — may involve a transfer of your data to a third country, including the United States. That transfer is made directly from your device to that service, under its own terms and its own transfer safeguards, rather than through any system of ours. We operate no servers and hold no copy of your information, so there is no onward international transfer by us. Google acts as an independent controller for a pass you save and is responsible for the safeguards covering it under the Google Privacy Policy.

## 5. How long we keep your information

We keep nothing ourselves, because we receive nothing. Anything you save to Google Wallet is kept by Google under its own retention practices — see below.

The information stored on your device remains there under your control until you delete an individual card in the App, clear the App's storage in your device settings, or uninstall the App. Uninstalling the App removes the local database and preferences.

If you have saved a card to Google Wallet, that pass lives in your Google Account and is **not** removed by deleting the card, clearing the App's data, or uninstalling the App. Google keeps it under its own retention practices until you delete it in the Google Wallet app. We cannot delete it for you, because we have no access to it.

Note that on Android the App disables cloud backup, so your cards are not copied to Google Drive. On iOS, if you have iCloud Backup enabled at the system level, your device backup is handled by Apple under Apple's own privacy policy.

If a card you saved contains another person's contact details and that person asks you to remove it, delete the pass in the Google Wallet app yourself — we have no access to it and cannot remove it for you. Please only put another person's details on a card with their permission.

If you wish to make a request or have any question about these rights, contact us at htbr.app@gmail.com. We will respond within 30 days.

## 7. Security

Because your information stays on your device unless you deliberately share it, it is not exposed to the risks that come with storing data on a server we operate — we operate none.

The App relies on the security model of your device's operating system: its database and preferences are held in the App's private storage area, which other apps cannot read on a non-compromised device.

The App opens no network connections for its own purposes: it has no analytics, no update checks, and no server of ours to call. Two things do involve the network, both at your initiative. External links you choose to open use HTTPS and are handled by your browser or the relevant app. Saving a card to Google Wallet is handed to Google Play services on your device, which transmits the pass to Google over its own encrypted connection; no credential or key of yours is involved. A saved pass also references our app icon by public HTTPS URL, which Google's servers fetch when rendering the pass — that request is made by Google, not by your device, and tells the host nothing about you. On Android, the App sets `allowBackup="false"`, so its data is excluded from Android's automatic cloud backup.

Protecting the device itself remains important. We recommend using a device passcode or biometric lock, since anyone with physical access to an unlocked device can open the App and read the cards stored in it.

No method of storage is 100% secure. While we strive to protect your information, we cannot guarantee absolute security.
